# The motion brief

A good brief is specific: what exists, the look, the one change, the limits, and how to check it. "Make it dynamic, go all out" is not a brief.
This extends [graphic briefs](../../non-resolve-production/references/graphic-briefs.md) (which cover one graphic on a recording) to whole motion pieces.

## The 8 parts

Parts 1-7 are the brief and stay the same for every piece in one brand (save them as `motion-brief.md`). Part 8 changes each time.

1. **The bar:** the quality level in one line, and "if it would not survive there, it is not done".
2. **Format:** width x height in pixels, exact seconds, loop or one-way, sound on or off.
3. **The one rule:** one hero object fills 60-70% of the frame and shows one satisfying change, then resets.
4. **Look:** background, depth (shadows, glass, highlights), real components, one or two accent colours as hex codes, the font, the hero word or number at 120 px or more, labels never under 34 px, at most five words on screen.
5. **Motion:** spring physics with a small settle (or `calm`), nothing linear, hold the finished state at least 1.2 s, one thing always alive, only one focus.
6. **Never:** grey placeholder bars, stock icons, emoji, clip art, dark or noisy ground when the brand is light, small objects floating in empty space, other brands' logos.
7. **Before you finish:** render a still every second, judge each as a design director, then again at 200 px wide, and fix anything generic, cheap, too small or empty.
8. **The scene:** one moment in two or three sentences: the hero object, the one change, the reset. If the idea has several moments, pick the strongest and list the rest as swap-in scenes.

Add an **originality** line: every element is original, never a copy of a real product's UI, icons or logos.

**Intake before writing it** (one batch, four questions at most): the moment (what changes, before and after), the exact words and numbers (real, never invented), the size (1080x960 tile, 1080x1350, 1080x1920, 1920x1080), and the brand if none was given. If something essential is missing, ask only for that and wait. With no brand, use the clearly labelled default: light ground `#F5F5F7`, soft layered shadows, frosted panels, one or two system accent colours, a tight heavy sans, "default, replace with your brand".

## From a reference you admire

1. **Break the video down for its moves**, not its pixels: the sequence of changes, the timing of each, what stays still, the one detail that sells it. Take timestamps.
2. **Take the look** from a website, a moodboard or a frame set: palette, type, depth, finish. Colours are read only where clearly legible; never guess a hex code.
3. **Write a scene-by-scene brief** in your own wording and your own brand, then build. A reference is a study, not a source: do not copy its scripts, assets, durations or marks.

## How to give notes

One change per round. "The headline runs past its box at 3 seconds. Fix only that. Keep the motion, the timing and the loop exactly as they are, and tell me what you changed." See [polish](./polish.md).
